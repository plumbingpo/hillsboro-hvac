The Benefits of Professional HVAC Maintenance

There is a short list of things homeowners can handle: changing filters on time, keeping the outdoor coil clear, leaving registers open, and checking the drain. The rest takes instruments, because the problems that leave you stranded look perfectly normal. A capacitor that has lost 20 percent of its rating looks just like a new one. Low charge shows up only in superheat and subcooling readings. Cracked heat exchangers, weak flame signals, marginal pressure switches, and amp draws closing in on the breaker's limit are all invisible until measured, and all of them cost less to fix the season before they fail.

Burners, flame proving, venting, and draft need annual instrumented verification because the failure mode is not just a cold house, it is combustion products where they do not belong. How to Prevent HVAC Maintenance Problems

A few simple habits now, like regular filter changes and seasonal tune-ups, can add years to your system's life. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Trim plants back from the outdoor unit and give the coil a gentle rinse every spring
  • Resist closing vents in spare rooms; the system is designed with all registers open
  • Schedule maintenance in early spring and early fall, ahead of the busy season and the extreme weather
  • If you have gas or other fuel-burning equipment, test your CO detectors every month
  • Replace your filter on a one- to three-month cycle; nothing else you do pays off more
  • Jot down any unusual sounds or run times, since they help your technician diagnose early
  • Flush the condensate line every spring so algae cannot build up and clog it
